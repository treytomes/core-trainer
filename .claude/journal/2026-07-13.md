# Journal Entry - July 13, 2026

## Theme: the transformer as a Turing tape — and the head-tracking ceiling

This session followed one thread end to end: the recurring puzzle of *why a full-adder is so hard to
train as a circuit, yet an LLM adds fine via chain-of-thought* — and where that leads.

## The reframing that started it

A fixed-depth transformer is bounded (≈ uniform TC⁰); a carry chain is inherently sequential and does not
fold into constant depth — which is exactly why the logic-network experiments
([LogicMlp](../../examples/CoreTrainer.Examples.LogicMlp/), DiffNand, CGP) walled on the parallel adder.
But autoregressive generation breaks that ceiling: each emitted token is a fresh forward pass that reads
the whole scratchpad, so the computation is *bounded per-step transducer × unbounded steps*, with the
**context window as the tape**. The carry chain that won't parallelize is trivial to *ripple* one step per
token.

That framed a ladder: prove a transformer can *execute* algorithms on its tape, climbing toward a
2-counter (Minsky) machine — which, being Turing-complete, would demonstrate universal computation over a
bounded tape.

## What we built

- **[CoreTrainer.Examples.AdderTape](../../examples/CoreTrainer.Examples.AdderTape/)** — a NoPE
  full-precision GELU transformer trained on binary-addition ripple-carry *tapes*, tested by **holding out
  length** (train 1–4 bit, test 5–8 bit). Length-held-out rules out memorization by construction — only a
  position-invariant step function transfers. NoPE (no positional embeddings) keeps the test valid:
  learned absolute positions past the training length would be untrained, failing for positional rather
  than algorithmic reasons.
- **[CoreTrainer.Examples.CopyTape](../../examples/CoreTrainer.Examples.CopyTape/)** — a copy machine
  (plain / reverse / zip modes) that isolates **head-tracking** from all computation, built after the
  adder pointed at head-tracking as the wall.
- Supporting machinery reused across both: batched generation, per-length exec-accuracy, checkpoint/resume,
  a format self-check, and a failure dump — the last two earned their keep repeatedly.

## The findings, in order

1. **The adder cliffed on held-out length** — both an indexed format (5b:55% → 8b:0%) and a rebuilt
   "streaming tape" format (queue-consumed operands, s-digit answer, run-out termination; 47/9/2/1). Two
   formats, two distinct failure *modes*, the same wall.

2. **The wall is head-tracking, not carry depth.** Failures were structural — dropped record slots, wrong
   operand-bit reads, lost step counts — not arithmetic. Trey's articulation nailed it: there are **two
   kinds of position** — *chain* position (which step; must be invariant; NoPE correctly removes) and
   *within-chunk* position (bit *k* of the operand; needed; NoPE stripped it too, and that broke). A Turing
   machine's control is `(state, head-position)`; a transformer has the state (on the tape) but **no
   explicit head-position register**.

3. **The copy probe measured the head-tracking limit cleanly.** Plain copy, tiny model (0.10 M, dim 64,
   2 layers), NoPE, train 4–12 bit / test 16–24 bit. Result, to convergence:

   | epoch | train-len | held-out 16b | 20b | 24b |
   |-------|-----------|--------------|-----|-----|
   | 1000  | 76%       | 1%  | 0% | 0% |
   | 2000  | 94%       | 2%  | 0% | 0% |
   | 3000  | **99.4%** | 3%  | 0% | 0% |

   Failure dumps showed the mechanism: the output *tracks the input for a prefix, then drifts* (e.g. 14/16
   bits correct, tail garbage). Head-tracking is **real, degrades with distance, and its reliable range is
   capped** — train-len saturated at ~100% while held-out stayed flat at ~2–4% and 20/24-bit never left
   zero. That's the overfitting-to-trained-lengths signature, not a pre-knee plateau.

## The conclusion — and why it matters

**NoPE did not remove the context-length limit; it rebuilt it from scratch in the attention dynamics.**
Trey's summary: *"The context length problem isn't gone; we just re-built it from scratch."* RoPE's length
ceiling lives explicitly in trained rotation frequencies; NoPE's lives implicitly in a learned,
induction-like position-reconstruction circuit whose finite precision caps its range just as hard — only
less visibly. The limit is a property of finite-precision *sequential position reconstruction*, not of any
particular positional encoding.

The load-bearing reframe: **reliable head-tracking distance = the effective (computationally usable)
context window.** Advertised context is the tape a model can *read*; reliable head-tracking distance is the
tape it can *compute on*. The second is smaller, and it is the real ceiling on the whole Turing-tape
ladder — every rung above copy needs at least this much, usually more (a counter machine moves the head
both directions).

## Open question for when we pull this together

Does **model size** raise the head-tracking cap, and *why*? The deciding experiment is a size sweep
(dim64/2L → dim128/4L → dim256/6L) plotting **reliable head-tracking distance vs parameter count**:

- grows ~linearly, keeps pace with length → **capacity-bound**: the ceiling is purchasable ("N params buys
  M steps of usable tape"), scaling is the lever.
- grows sub-linearly, asymptotes (cliff moves out, shape unchanged) → **mechanism-bound**: the
  reconstruction circuit has a per-step error floor scale only dents, and an explicit head anchor is
  needed — a *content-based moving cursor* (head-position as a tape symbol the model advances, keeping
  NoPE) being the more interesting fix than reverting to RoPE (which carries its own ceiling).

Metric discipline for the sweep: watch the **held-out correct-prefix length**, not train-len exec-accuracy
— a bigger model memorizes trained lengths harder and improves the wrong number.
